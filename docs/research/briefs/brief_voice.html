Proposal 2 · Quick brief

Offline Voice-Based Khata Entry in Hindi, Marathi and English for Kirana Stores

Research Methodology · what we are proposing, in plain words
In one line: can a free speech model running offline on a cheap Android phone correctly catch the name, amount and credit/repayment from a shopkeeper speaking mixed Hindi, Marathi and English, and is that faster than typing?

1The problem

Typing is slow

At a busy counter, finding the customer and typing the amount takes time. It's harder still for people not comfortable typing.

No internet

Good speech recognition usually runs in the cloud. Shops lose network, and our app is offline-first.

Mixed languages

People say "Ramesh ka 250 ka udhaar add karo", with Hindi, Marathi and English in one sentence.

For money, only three details matter: who, how much, credit or repayment. If "dhai sau" (250) is heard as "do sau" (200), the account is ₹50 off. So we judge by those details, not by word errors.

2What we are proposing

A "speak entry" button in SmartOps that works fully offline:

Example: "Sunanda tai ne saade teen hazaar jama kele" becomes Sunanda Patil · ₹3,500 · Repayment, and the shopkeeper taps Save.

3What we compare

Baseline

Cloud service

Google speech-to-text. Needs internet. Our "best case" to compare with.

Offline

Whisper on the phone

Free OpenAI model, "small" size, compressed to 8-bit so it fits on a cheap phone.

Offline + hint

Whisper + name list

We give it the shop's customer names first, so rare names like "Sunanda" are heard right.

4How we will test it

  1. Record about 360 clips: 12 speakers (4 of us, 4 friends, 4 shopkeepers) × 30 short commands, read from cards or said in their own words. Names are made up, and we know the right answer for every clip.
  2. Run every clip through all 3 setups, so it's the same speech for each, a fair comparison.
  3. Check: is the amount right? the name? the whole entry? How long did the phone take?
  4. Usability check: 8–10 people do 5 entries by voice and 5 by typing. We time them and they fill a 10-question SUS form. Half start with voice, half with typing.

5What we expect to show (hypotheses)

#In plain wordsTestWe are wrong if…
H1Cloud gets more whole entries right than offline.McNemarno real difference, which would be good news for offline
H2Giving the customer-name list improves name accuracy.McNemarthe list doesn't help

McNemar, in one breath

Same clip, two setups. Ignore clips both got right or both got wrong, and check if one wins the disagreements far more than 50-50.

Wilcoxon, in one breath

For each person: voice time minus typing time. Check whether "voice faster" clearly outweighs "typing faster". No bell curve needed.

6What we expect to find (expectations, not results)

  • Cloud will be somewhat better. The question is how big the gap is. A small gap makes offline practical, especially with the confirm screen.
  • Most mistakes will be in names and number words like "dhai", "saade", "paune". Mixed-language and noisy-shop clips will be harder.
  • The name list should clearly improve name accuracy.
  • Voice should be faster for most people. Some may prefer typing for privacy, since saying a customer's debt aloud can be awkward.

Pitch

"Shopkeepers find typing khata entries slow at a busy counter. Speaking is faster, but speech recognition usually needs internet. 9Tough questions, quick answers

"Why not just use Google's cloud?"
We do, as the comparison. But the app must work without internet, so the real question is how close offline gets.
"360 clips is tiny."
It's small for training but fine for testing. Every clip goes through every setup, so comparisons are paired, and McNemar only needs the disagreements.
"Why only 8–10 people?"
Each person uses both methods, so we compare people with themselves. That's a normal size for a first usability check.
"Doesn't the confirm screen defeat the purpose?"
No. Checking one line and tapping Save is still much faster than typing, and it keeps money entries safe.
"Why rules, not ChatGPT, to pick out details?"
Rules work offline, are fast, and are easy to check and explain.
